Our firm faces foreign exchange exposure on an upcoming accounts receivable of EUR 17,500,000 due in 182 days from an Irish data-center operator, currently valued at $19,547,500 at a spot rate of 1.1170. If the euro depreciates before settlement, conversion proceeds will drop significantly—such as falling to $18,375,000 if the spot rate hits 1.0500—directly eroding operating margins. To mitigate this risk, we evaluated three hedging families: forward contracts, which lock in a theoretical forward rate ($F_0$ of 1.1265 yielding $19,712,875) at the expense of upside participation; money market hedges, which synthetically replicate the forward payout through cross-border borrowing and lending; and options strategies, such as buying a EUR put option (strike at spot 1.1170 for a $0.0194 premium) to establish a downside floor of $19,208,000 while retaining upside flexibility.Our implementation plan spans Stages 2 through 5, beginning with finalized model inputs ($R_{USD} = 4.285\%$, $R_{FC} = 2.590\%$), followed by scenario modeling of terminal cash flows, executing the optimal hedge based on risk tolerance, and concluding with a post-audit review. We assume stable money market liquidity, constant risk-free rates, and frictionless derivative execution over the 182-day horizon.We hypothesize that utilizing a structured Put Option Strategy or collar will maximize risk-adjusted value by shielding the firm from severe downward currency shocks while preserving operational upside in a volatile tech export market. This hypothesis would be falsified if significant positive euro momentum materializes and holding an unhedged or option-based position consistently underperforms a strict forward lock by a margin wider than our cost of capital, or if option time-decay erosion outweighs the realized protection benefits.
