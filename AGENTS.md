# AGENTS.md

## Purpose

This repository is the personal resume and portfolio of a third-year accounting
student at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa (UH Mānoa). The repository may
contain resume materials, accounting projects, data-analysis work, coursework,
career-preparation materials, and portfolio content.

AI agents working in this repository should help maintain a professional,
accurate, organized, and career-focused portfolio.

## Core Instructions

### 1. Accuracy

- Never invent work experience, education, certifications, awards, skills,
  technical abilities, projects, or accomplishments.
- Do not exaggerate the user's responsibilities or level of expertise.
- Clearly distinguish between completed work, ongoing work, planned work, and
  hypothetical examples.
- When information is missing, use a placeholder rather than making up details.
- Preserve factual accuracy in accounting, tax, statistics, and data-analysis
  work.

### 2. Accounting and Tax Work

When assisting with accounting or tax projects:

- Explain concepts clearly and accurately.
- Show calculations when appropriate.
- Identify assumptions used in calculations.
- Distinguish accounting rules from tax rules.
- For tax-related content, identify the applicable tax year when relevant.
- Flag areas where tax law may have changed or where professional verification
  is necessary.
- Do not present educational examples as personalized tax advice.
- Use appropriate accounting terminology and conventions.

### 3. Data Analysis

When assisting with data-analysis projects:

- Explain the methodology used.
- Identify relevant assumptions.
- Keep calculations reproducible whenever possible.
- Clearly distinguish correlation from causation.
- Explain statistical results in plain language as well as technical language.
- Do not manipulate or selectively present data to produce a preferred
  conclusion.
- Preserve the original data whenever practical and document transformations.

### 4. Career Preparation

When helping with resumes, cover letters, interviews, networking messages,
or other career materials:

- Keep the writing professional, confident, and authentic.
- Emphasize demonstrated skills rather than making unsupported claims.
- Avoid unnecessarily corporate, exaggerated, or overly generic language.
- Prioritize clarity and conciseness.
- Tailor materials to the specific position when information about the
  position is available.
- Do not claim the user has experience they have not actually obtained.

### 5. Resume Rules

When editing the resume:

- Keep formatting consistent.
- Prefer strong action verbs.
- Focus bullet points on actions, skills, and measurable results when
  measurements are genuinely available.
- Do not fabricate metrics.
- Avoid unnecessary first-person pronouns.
- Keep descriptions concise.
- Preserve factual information unless explicitly asked to change it.
- Use professional terminology appropriate for accounting and business roles.

## Writing Style

The preferred tone is:

- Professional
- Clear
- Natural
- Confident without being boastful
- Concise
- Student-professional rather than overly formal

Avoid:

- Buzzword-heavy writing
- Excessive corporate jargon
- Generic claims such as "hard-working individual" unless supported by context
- Overly complicated sentences
- Artificially sophisticated vocabulary
- Statements that sound exaggerated for a student resume

## File Management

Before creating or modifying files:

1. Check the existing repository structure.
2. Preserve existing work unless the user explicitly asks for deletion or
   replacement.
3. Keep related materials organized in their appropriate directories.
4. Use descriptive filenames.
5. Avoid committing temporary, generated, or system files.
6. Do not expose private information unnecessarily.

## Privacy

Do not add or expose:

- Passwords
- API keys
- Authentication tokens
- Private credentials
- Personal identification numbers
- Sensitive financial information
- Private documents that are not intended for public viewing

Use placeholders for sensitive information when necessary.

## Git Practices

Prefer small, logical changes.

Commit messages should clearly describe the change, for example:

- `Add updated accounting resume`
- `Add data analysis portfolio project`
- `Update internship experience`
- `Improve portfolio README`

Do not commit secrets, system files, temporary files, or unnecessary generated
files.
