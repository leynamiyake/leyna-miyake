# CLAUDE.md

## Project Context

This repository belongs to a third-year accounting student at the University
of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa (UH Mānoa) who works at the NetLab and is working toward
a future CPA license.

The primary purpose of this repository is to maintain a professional resume
and portfolio that demonstrates academic development, accounting knowledge,
data-analysis abilities, technical skills, projects, and career preparation.

## Background to Remember

When assisting with this repository, remember that the user:

- Is a third-year accounting student at UH Mānoa.
- Works at the NetLab.
- Is pursuing accounting as a career.
- Has a long-term goal of earning a CPA license.
- Is interested in accounting and tax.
- Is developing practical professional experience alongside their education.
- May use the repository to showcase accounting, data-analysis, coursework,
  and other business-related projects.

Do not assume additional qualifications or experience unless they are explicitly
documented in the repository or provided by the user.

## Goals

Prioritize helping the user:

1. Build a professional accounting-focused portfolio.
2. Present their resume clearly and accurately.
3. Demonstrate practical accounting and analytical skills.
4. Document projects in a way that employers can understand.
5. Prepare for accounting internships and future accounting roles.
6. Develop toward a CPA career.
7. Maintain a clean and professional GitHub presence.

## Tone

Use a tone that is:

- Professional
- Clear
- Approachable
- Confident
- Authentic
- Appropriate for a college student entering the accounting profession

The writing should sound like a capable accounting student rather than an
executive or marketing department.

Avoid:

- Excessive buzzwords
- Overly formal language
- Artificially impressive claims
- Generic AI-sounding language
- Unsubstantiated claims of expertise
- Repetitive descriptions

## Resume and Career Materials

When editing resume or career materials:

- Never invent accomplishments.
- Never invent numbers or performance metrics.
- Never claim certifications that have not been earned.
- Never claim accounting or tax experience that has not been documented.
- Highlight transferable skills when appropriate.
- Use action-oriented language.
- Keep descriptions concise.
- Prefer specific evidence over generic adjectives.

When tailoring a resume to a job description, identify genuine connections
between the user's background and the position rather than adding keywords
without evidence.

## Accounting and Tax

For accounting questions and projects:

- Prioritize technical accuracy.
- Explain the reasoning behind calculations.
- Use appropriate accounting terminology.
- Show journal entries, calculations, or formulas when useful.
- Clearly identify assumptions.

For tax-related questions:

- Specify the relevant tax year when applicable.
- Recognize that tax laws and thresholds can change.
- Distinguish educational explanations from professional tax advice.
- Verify current tax rules when the task requires current information.

## Data Analysis

For data-analysis projects:

- Keep methods transparent and reproducible.
- Explain statistical results in understandable language.
- State important assumptions.
- Avoid overstating conclusions.
- Distinguish statistical association from causation.
- Document meaningful data-cleaning or transformation steps.

## GitHub Presentation

Favor a clean, employer-friendly repository.

README files should generally answer:

1. What is this project?
2. Why was it created?
3. What skills or concepts does it demonstrate?
4. What tools or technologies were used?
5. What was learned or accomplished?
6. Where can the relevant files or results be found?

Keep the repository organized and avoid unnecessary files.

## Privacy and Security

Never commit:

- Passwords
- API keys
- Access tokens
- Credentials
- Private financial information
- Sensitive personal information
- Confidential employer information

Use placeholders or environment variables when appropriate.

## Before Making Changes

Before modifying an existing file:

1. Read the relevant file.
2. Understand its current purpose and structure.
3. Preserve useful existing content.
4. Make the smallest reasonable change.
5. Check for consistency with the rest of the repository.

When uncertain about a factual detail concerning the user's background, ask the
user rather than inventing an answer.
